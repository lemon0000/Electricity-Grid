Priority-Aware Firm and Flexible Capacity
Quantification for Large-Load Interconnection
Shaoze Li 1, Bohang Fang 1, Junjie Qin 2, and Cong Chen 1,∗
Abstract—We study how to unlock existing transmission capac-
ity to accelerate large-load interconnection under differentiated
quality-of-service (QoS) requirements. We establish an efficient
framework to quantify firm and flexible hosting capacities and
assess marginal values of grid upgrades. Specifically, we define
multiple flexible capacity tiers with differentiated QoS limits and
design a two-layer priority rule to allocate curtailment among
tiers. We then formulate a mixed-integer chance-constrained
model to jointly quantify firm and flexible capacities under QoS
and priority constraints. To improve computational efficiency,
we derive an equivalent aggregate formulation that eliminates
individual curtailment variables and priority binaries without
changing the feasible capacity set. Numerical results show the
computational advantage of aggregation. Multi-tier flexible ca-
pacity increases total admitted capacity by 45.0% over the firm-
only hosting capacity.
Index Terms—Large-load interconnection, flexible capacity,
curtailment priority.
I. INTRODUCTION
The rapid growth of large electricity loads, including AI
data centers, is placing increasing pressure on transmission
grid interconnection [1]. However, transmission grid expansion
is slow and capital-intensive, making it difficult to keep pace
with large-load interconnection requests. This paper focuses
on accommodating this demand surge before network up-
grades while meeting differentiated quality-of-service (QoS)
requirements. Specifically, we consider multi-tier flexible in-
terconnection, where different tiers have distinct QoS limits.
Unlike firm capacity, which must be served under all planning
conditions, flexible capacity (also known as non-firm load
service) allows curtailment subject to agreed QoS limits.
Existing studies demonstrate the benefits of such flexibility
for load growth in U.S. power systems [2], distribution-level
interconnection [3], and data-center siting in transmission
networks [4]. However, large-load customers may differ in
their QoS requirements, both in terms of allowed curtailment
depth and the probability of exceeding that depth. These
studies do not explicitly consider multiple flexible capacity
tiers with differentiated QoS requirements.
Another challenge is how to allocate curtailment when
multiple flexible tiers share a bus. Their QoS limits do not
specify how curtailment should be distributed among them.
Priority-based allocation provides one efficient approach and

jointly admit while satisfying network and QoS constraints.
Our contributions addressing these gaps are twofold. First,
we develop a two-layer curtailment priority rule and formulate
a unified mixed-integer chance-constrained model that maxi-
mizes the preference-weighted firm and flexible interconnec-
tion capacity subject to network, priority, and QoS limits. We
then establish an exact aggregate reformulation that eliminates
individual curtailment variables and priority binaries without
changing the feasible set of capacity decisions. Second, to
improve computational efficiency and guide grid upgrades, we
first approximate the aggregate model by a linear program
(LP) whose feasible solutions provably satisfy the QoS limits,
then derive marginal upgrade values from the dual variables
that indicate how much additional hosting capacity can be
unlocked by the upgrade in transmission or generation ca-
pacity. Numerical results on the PJM 5-bus system show that
aggregation reduces solution time from 5801.5 s to 1.56 s
while preserving the optimal objective value. On the IEEE RTS
73-bus system, results show that multi-tier flexible capacity
increases total admitted capacity by 45.0% over the firm-only
hosting capacity.
The paper is organized as follows. Section II defines the
capacity products and formulates the individual model under
the two-layer curtailment priority rule. Section III derives the
equivalent aggregate model and the LP approximation, whose
duals give marginal upgrade values. The experiments are given
in Section IV.
II. INDIVIDUALCAPACITYQUANTIFICATION

model for capacity quantification. The grid planner performs
this quantification to determine firm and flexible interconnec-
tion capacities. In the next section, we derive an equivalent
aggregate model and the LP approximation to improve com-
putational efficiency and obtain marginal upgrade information.

B. The Two-Layer Curtailment Priority Rule
Equation (1) specifies the QoS requirement of each flexible
product but does not determine how curtailment is allocated.
We therefore adopt a priority rule for products sharing a bus.
Specifically, we adopt a two-layer rule: the allowed curtailment
depths of all flexible products are used before any product is
curtailed beyond its allowed depth. The first layer is the within-
depth layer, with tranche sizesa ik :=δ kcik; the second layer
is the exceedance layer, with tranche sizesb ik := (1−δ k)cik.
Within each layer, less reliable tiers are curtailed before more
reliable ones. Next, a small example of two products shows
the curtailment priority. Fig. 1 illustrates this order for two
products, with the bus index omitted. Curtailment first uses
the 5 MW within-depth tranche of product 2, then the 2 MW
within-depth tranche of product 1. Only after both within-
depth tranches are exhausted does it use the 5 MW exceedance
tranche of product 2, followed by the 8 MW of product 1.

V. CONCLUSION
This paper studies large-load interconnection with firm
capacity and multiple flexible capacity tiers under a two-layer
curtailment priority rule. We show that the individual and
aggregate quantification models have the same feasible set of
capacity decisions. This removes the priority binaries without
changing the optimal value, thus supporting the efficient
quantification of hosting capacity. The CVaR approximation
further yields an LP that satisfies the sampled individual QoS
limits and provides dual information for screening possible
grid upgrades.
